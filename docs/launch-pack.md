# Launching a glass-transfer service pilot

This is a proposed UK launch for one fictional glass-processing cell. Prices, volumes and targets below are test assumptions. The sector choice draws on a published application where the customer needed to position glass accurately without marking it. [Dip-Tech case](https://www.alwayse.com/success-stories/high-tech-solution-for-handling-glass/)

## First customers and routes to market

Approach plants with an identifiable transfer station, recurring interruptions, an accessible work-order history and a maintenance lead able to arrange access. An operations manager can sponsor discovery. The budget holder needs to confirm how much of each lost hour can be recovered elsewhere on the line. Where interruptions are rare or the plant restores service quickly from stocked spares, retaining the current arrangement may be the better buying decision.

| Route | Buying alternative and proposed incentive | Question before approaching the customer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct to a processor | The plant can retain in-house maintenance, buy spare units or commission a repair as needed. Offer a fixed-price trial that gives procurement a documented choice at close-out. | Which budget bears the cost, and would the buyer pay to document and improve this station? |
| Through a glass-line integrator | A processor may already have a whole-line support contract. The integrator could retain the account and first-line triage, with specialist deck support as a subcontracted service. | Who owns each fault, customer update and invoice when a stoppage crosses equipment boundaries? |
| Through a distributor | A distributor can continue to sell replacement parts. Offer an agreed fee for qualifying a site or delivering a visit if it has qualified staff and coverage. | Does the fee cover the partner's time and travel, and does the customer accept this route? |

Agree one contact owner per account, record introductions with permission and cost any partner payment before quoting. The manufacturer publicly supports distributors with training and technical assistance. These pilot incentives would need separate agreement. ## Customer offer sheet

**Eight-week transfer-cell review · £1,150 ex VAT · illustrative fixed pilot price**

For one existing glass-transfer cell at your site, we will agree which equipment the review covers, document a recent stoppage and assess whether planned support would pay for itself. The equipment remains yours during the pilot.

| Timing | What you receive | What we need from you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before booking | Written scope, price, named lead and agreed access window | A named buyer, maintenance contact, asset identifier and permission to review relevant records |
| Weeks 1–2 | One booked site visit, a register of covered units, a condition note and a baseline from available records | Site induction, access approval and a shift representative to walk through one recent interruption |
| Weeks 3–7 | A shared incident template, remote review of logged events and a written record of unresolved causes | Scheduled operating hours, event times and changes in workload or line configuration |
| Week 8 | A close-out meeting, reconciled findings and a priced continuation option if both sides can support it | A buyer's decision to proceed, revise or close, with the main reason recorded |

**Service boundaries.** The service lead owns the report and coordinates visits. A competent service engineer records the condition of accessible covered equipment. The integrator handles faults on adjacent machinery. The site authorises access and return to use. Parts, modifications, additional visits, glass damage and compensation for lost production require separate written scope. The parties agree the review schedule before booking. The pilot makes no uptime commitment.

**Pricing check.** The example allows £350 for the visit including travel, £300 for analysis and £150 for coordination. This leaves £350 before overhead and partner payments. A £200 referral or delivery fee would reduce this remainder to £150. Verify the local delivery cost and avoid paying twice for the same visit. The workbench separately models a three-year service including equipment supply. [Contract model](../site/model.js)

## Getting from discovery to paid use

Use a single six-week recruiting cohort, then allow eight weeks for the pilot and one week for its decision. Assign each site one route and track losses by stage. These targets are trial design choices and have no historical baseline.

| Entry or conversion | Illustrative target | Evidence counted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eligible sites identified | 12 distinct sites | Identifiable station, contact route and likely budget owner |
| Discovery conversations completed | 6 of 12 | Named sponsor and one recent interruption discussed |
| Sites ready for a priced scope | 3 of 6 | Baseline records, access and deliverable boundary reviewed |
| Written offers issued | 2 of 3 | Buyer receives fee, exclusions, delivery owner and decision date |
| Paid pilots booked | 1 of 2 | Purchase order or signed paid scope |
| Priced continuation decided | 1 decision per completed pilot | Buyer records accept, revise or decline and a reason |

During discovery, ask the operator and maintenance lead to reconstruct the same event independently. Ask the buyer to compare the pilot price with the next-best use of that budget. During the trial, reconcile stop times against work orders and compare attributed hours per 1,000 operating hours with a comparable baseline. Track exclusions, partner cost and missing records. One paid trial supports further testing. Broader rollout calls for evidence from additional sites and delivery routes. [Field method and handover](field-guide.md)
