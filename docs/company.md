# Alwayse Engineering and the opportunity for lifecycle services

Public-source review, 23 November 2026.

Alwayse manufactures ball transfer units in Birmingham. An array of these units forms a deck across which operators can move and position a load in several directions. The company sells individual components, builds handling systems around them and supports customers in the field. UKRI names Alwayse and Aston University in project 10146336. Its abstract connects the move towards supplying complete solutions with market research and business model innovation. [Alwayse company site](https://www.alwayse.com/) · [UKRI project record](https://gtr.ukri.org/projects?ref=10146336)

**The commercial question for this prototype** is whether a site would pay a recurring fee for an agreed level of support, and whether the manufacturer and its delivery partners could fulfil that promise profitably. Alwayse already markets planned maintenance and tailored service plans. The public evidence establishes a starting point for testing an offer. Contract terms, customer demand and delivery economics require primary research. [Maintenance services](https://www.alwayse.com/ball-transfer-maintenance-services/)

## How to test the proposed offer

1. **Reconstruct one recent incident.** Ask an operator, the maintenance lead and the service partner to walk through the same event from detection to return to service. Compare their timings with work orders and shift records. Record disagreements and missing evidence.
2. **Check who would buy it.** Give procurement an itemised example covering the asset, visit allowance, parts, response window, exclusions and fee. Ask which budget would pay, who could approve a trial and which alternative they would choose at that price.
3. **Check who could deliver it.** Have service staff and the local partner cost travel, planned work, call-outs, parts and any credits. Test a difficult month as well as a typical one. Rework the offer if the fee acceptable to the customer cannot cover the provider's costs.
4. **Agree an evaluation before the pilot.** Define scheduled hours, attributable downtime and who signs off an incident. Compare like operating windows, track changes in workload and retain reasons for any exclusions. End the trial with an explicit decision to expand, redesign or stop.
