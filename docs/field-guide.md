# Pilot field guide and handover

This is a proposed field method for a fictional glass-handling line. No interview, observation, willingness-to-pay test or commercial pilot has been conducted for this demonstration. Roles below are proposed accountabilities. Replace the scenario, owners and dates with agreed customer information before using the method in a real project.

## 1. Start with an interruption

Choose one handling station with an identifiable production boundary, an operations sponsor and enough logged running time to establish a baseline. Invite an operator, a maintainer and the person who would approve a service contract. Work across shifts where practical. Invite the distributor or equipment integrator if it owns the service relationship.

| Role | Opening question | Evidence to request with permission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shift operator | Walk me through the last time material stopped moving here. What happened during recovery? | Event timestamp, line identifier, planned runtime, separate planned breaks |
| Maintenance lead | Who diagnosed the issue? Where did people wait for access, technical advice or spares? | Work order, stock record, response and restoration times |
| Buyer | Which budget currently absorbs the interruption and repair? Who would authorise a recurring fee? | Prior purchase process, acceptable scope, decision owner, willingness to consider a priced trial |
| Service or channel partner | Who receives the call, supplies the unit and approves an engineer visit? What happens out of hours? | Coverage boundary, dispatch capacity, logistics cost and contract exclusions |

Agree who can collect and view records, the lawful permissions and retention period with the site sponsor and university supervisor. Obtain permission before recording or quoting anyone. Offer written input or a remote interview and invite staff outside the most senior group. Observe through the site's induction and approved viewing arrangements. A named site authority retains control of access, isolations and return to operation.

For each observation, capture **source, date, reported event, supporting record, conflicting accounts, unresolved question, owner and next review**. Use role aliases in a shareable copy and keep raw company records in an approved system. Avoid adding identifying details to the public demo.

## 2. Convert evidence into a service requirement

| Fictional observation | Proposed requirement | Delivery and ownership | Test and decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A shift team loses time identifying which unit was installed. | A maintainer can identify the station and installed unit at the point of reporting. | Engineering maintains a station register. Site maintenance verifies it on commissioning and replacement. | Ask two maintainers on different shifts to identify the unit from a sample record. Revise ambiguous fields before pricing the support. |
| A buyer cannot distinguish waiting time from repair time on an invoice. | The service review separates response, waiting for site access and restoration. | Service operations records timestamps. Customer operations confirms access exceptions. | Reconcile three events jointly. Resolve disagreement on ownership before promising a response credit. |
| An engineer thinks a spare will prevent downtime but the buyer doubts the saving. | Propose replenishment ownership and a stock target after observing actual drawdown. | Stores owns inventory. Finance agrees carrying cost and obsolescence treatment. | Compare holding cost with historical delay cost and test the money at risk if the saving is zero. |

Facilitate a 45-minute workshop. Spend ten minutes reconstructing an event, ten locating delays and challenging assumptions, fifteen drawing the proposed service response, and ten agreeing an owner and the next test. Give each participant an independent chance to challenge the proposal. Keep a dated record of feedback, resulting change and how the customer will confirm it.

## 3. Specify a measurable pilot

1. Agree the station, shift coverage, dates and operating-hours denominator. Assign an owner to planned time, unplanned interruptions, excluded events and spare purchases. Reconcile overlapping events before aggregation.
2. Calculate lost hours per 1,000 operating hours as verified unplanned hours divided by operating hours, multiplied by 1,000. Record missing logs as unknown. Collect supplier delivery costs, site spend, labour assumptions and the consequence per lost hour separately.
3. Capture baseline before changing the service. Track workload, glass mix, concurrent maintenance and line modifications during comparison. Include another comparable station if its team agrees and its records support a fair comparison.
4. Agree thresholds and decision rights with the customer and finance before the trial. Record who may pause for site safety, data quality, unacceptable cost or service coverage. Hold a joint close-out whether the figures improve or deteriorate.
5. Invite the buyer to approve, revise or decline a priced follow-on scope. Record actual behaviour with permission. A positive workshop comment has lower evidential weight than a signed scope or paid trial.

| Stage | Months | Evidence and accountable owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery | 1–3 | Associate and site lead agree target station, research permissions, baseline definition and capability gaps. |
| Co-design | 4–6 | Service lead and customer buyer accept a shared blueprint, exceptions and proposed measures. |
| Commercial preparation | 7–9 | Finance and engineering cost resource constraints, test downside and verify who can actually fulfil the offer. |
| Pilot delivery | 10–12 | Site sponsor and service manager review event-level evidence, customer feedback and stop criteria on agreed dates. |
| Launch decision | 13–15 | Commercial lead records contract interest, pricing approval and operational capacity. A failed gate returns the offer for revision. |
| Handover | 16–18 | Internal process owner runs a second case unaided. Academic and business supervisors agree a review date and research-release permissions. |

## 4. Prepare the offer and internal capability

Start with a single site that can share reliable records and name a decision maker. Invite the equipment integrator or distributor when it owns contact with the end user. Use the same evidence requirements when testing a second segment or route to market.

**Buyer proposition.** “We propose to trial a service on one transfer station. We will agree what constitutes an interruption, clarify who supplies spares and compare the total service cost with your current arrangement. At close-out, you can review the records, price and delivery responsibilities before deciding whether to continue.” Keep any cost or performance estimate conditional until verified.

**Technical handover card.** Record the station ID, installed unit ID, access window, monitoring owner, stock owner, reporting route, event start and stop definitions, dispatch hours and exclusions. Record who approves a changed component or return to use under site procedures. Feed access difficulties and incomplete configuration records into the next product design review.

| Capability to test | Likely owner | Readiness evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identify installed units and control configuration changes | Engineering lead | Versioned station register verified at the site |
| Collect and review usable service events | Service operations lead | Small sample reconciled with the customer and a named escalation route |
| Set price and control loss exposure | Finance lead | Cost-to-serve model including credits, travel, stock and constrained capacity |
| Communicate and deliver a scoped offer | Commercial and service leads | Approved sales sheet, dependencies, handover card and delivery ownership |
| Sustain the method beyond the KTP | Internal process owner | Owned templates, booked review and a colleague's independent case walkthrough |

Run a teach-back with a fresh fictional case. Give a colleague the templates, an ambiguous incident log and a different travel cost. Ask them to identify a customer need, check source evidence, price a service, explain a downside and decide the next test. Record which decision they completed independently, the one that needed help and the corresponding change to the training material. Set the next review with the internal owner.

## 5. Evaluate, publish and develop practice

**Research question.** Which activities in a scoped industrial transfer service change recorded interruption hours per 1,000 operating hours? How do operators, maintainers and buyers explain any change and its value?

**Research method.** Combine comparable baseline and trial windows with role-based interviews, the blueprint changelog and a finance review. Document missingness, competing explanations and areas of disagreement. Report replacement units and distance travelled per visit alongside cost. A before-and-after case can generate and refine hypotheses. Causal claims require a design that addresses alternative explanations.

**Outputs.** Produce a short internal case with methods, unresolved assumptions, commercial findings and transfer checklist. Review findings with participants where appropriate. Agree confidentiality, consent, authorship and release with both partners before a conference abstract or journal paper. A public conference case or paper would follow the work and review.

**Continuing development.** Agree a development objective with the academic supervisor, such as facilitating a service-design workshop or costing a contract. Observe an experienced colleague, lead a session, request feedback and record one change for the next iteration with the business mentor.
